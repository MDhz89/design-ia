# Anti-AI patterns

## Purpose

Use this document as a constraint during planning, implementation, and review.
It prevents common patterns that make AI-generated landing pages feel generic.

## Do not generate by default

- A centered hero composed of a badge, headline, paragraph, and two buttons.
- Purple-to-blue gradients as an automatic visual identity. Existing brand
  colors may still be used deliberately when they are supported by the product.
- Random glowing or blurred background orbs.
- Decorative grids, noise, or particles without semantic purpose.
- Three identical feature cards in a row.
- A card for every piece of content.
- Excessively rounded containers and pill-shaped controls.
- Icons inside colored circles for every feature.
- Repeated sections that use the same two-column layout.
- Floating screenshots with arbitrary rotation or perspective.
- Generic stock photography or abstract AI illustrations.
- Fake logos, testimonials, metrics, awards, or user counts.
- Sections added only to make the page longer.
- Animations that delay access to content or compete for attention.

## Avoid generic copy

Do not use unsupported or interchangeable phrases such as:

- "Transform your workflow."
- "Take your business to the next level."
- "Everything you need in one place."
- "Powerful, simple, and intuitive."
- "Built for modern teams."
- "Revolutionize the way you work."

Replace them with concrete statements about the product's actual users,
workflow, constraints, and outcomes.

## Review questions

- Could the same page be used for an unrelated SaaS product by changing its name?
- Does each section introduce distinct evidence or merely repeat a claim?
- Are visual treatments connected to content and interaction?
- Is the product visible, understandable, and believable?
- Can any section or decoration be removed without losing meaning?
